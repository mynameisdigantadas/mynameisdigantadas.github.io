involved. A policy is only useful when it describes the real service.
Check the live implementation, the content owner's records, the relevant
licence, and any agreement that applies. If the facts are uncertain,
pause publication or reuse until the responsible person can verify them.

Use clear language at the point where a visitor needs the information. A
general README should not be the only place where a critical restriction
appears. For example, a download page should identify important file
conditions, a form should explain why information is requested, and a
media page should show attribution or licence details close to the
material. Keep notices understandable and avoid hiding important
conditions in dense text.

Assign responsibility for this area to a named role, even if one person
currently performs several roles. The responsible person should know
where source files and permissions are stored, how to receive a report,
who can approve a change, and how to escalate a serious issue. Limit
administrative access to people who need it, and remove access promptly
when responsibilities change.

Review the area after a significant event, including a redesign, new
third-party integration, change of hosting provider, new audience, new
monetisation method, reported infringement, privacy complaint, security
incident, or change in law. Record what was checked, what was changed,
who approved it, and whether visitors need to be notified. A short,
accurate record is more useful than an unverified claim of compliance.

If a visitor challenges a decision relating to 📆 review cycle and
policy ownership, respond calmly and focus on verifiable facts. Identify
the relevant policy, explain the available review route, and avoid
disclosing another person's private information. Do not threaten a
complainant or promise an outcome before the facts and applicable rules
have been considered.

Do not assume that a technical control alone solves this issue. A robots
directive is not access control, a footer notice is not a signed
licence, a backup is not a privacy policy, and a search ranking is not a
quality certification. Combine appropriate technical measures with
accurate notices, trained editors, documented permissions, and a
practical way for visitors to ask questions.

Where the website does not currently provide the feature discussed in
this section, do not imply that it does. Mark the feature as unavailable
or planned, and publish the relevant terms before enabling it. A future
feature should receive a privacy, security, accessibility, copyright,
and support review before launch.

Keep public statements proportionate. Avoid absolute promises such as
'completely secure', 'always available', 'guaranteed top ranking', or
'all content is owned by us'. State what is actually done, what
limitations remain, and how visitors can report a problem. Honest limits
help visitors make informed decisions and reduce misunderstandings.

## New page publication workflow

1.  Define the visitor need and the intended educational outcome.
    Confirm that the page has a distinct purpose and is not simply a
    duplicate of an existing page.

2.  Draft original content. Attribute factual sources, distinguish
    opinion from fact, and check that examples are safe, current, and
    understandable.

3.  Verify rights for every image, icon, font, video, code sample,
    dataset, and quotation. Store permission or licence evidence before
    publication.

4.  Review privacy and security. Remove secrets, personal data, internal
    comments, debug output, and unpublished configuration.

5.  Review accessibility, mobile layout, spelling, links, metadata,
    structured data, and page speed. Check that the page title and
    description accurately reflect visible content.

6.  Publish with a review date where useful. Monitor reports and correct
    errors promptly.

## Handling a copyright report

1.  Record the date received, sender's contact details, page URL, work
    identified, and requested action. Restrict access to the complaint
    record.

2.  Check whether the sender appears to be the rights holder or an
    authorised representative. Ask for clarification if the notice is
    incomplete.

3.  Compare the material with the identified work and inspect the
    relevant permissions, licences, and publication history.

4.  Consider whether the material is third-party content, user-submitted
    content, licensed content, or original PUBLIC-WEBSITE content. Do not
    assume ownership based solely on where it appears.

5.  Where appropriate, temporarily restrict the material while reviewing
    the claim. Follow applicable law and any required notice or
    counter-notice process.

6.  Document the decision, notify relevant parties where appropriate,
    and retain only the records needed for legitimate purposes.

## Responding to a privacy concern

1.  Identify the affected page, data type, and whether the information
    is currently public or accessible through a third-party service.

2.  Do not repeat sensitive information in public replies. Restrict
    access to the report and involve the privacy contact.

3.  Assess whether immediate removal, access restriction, correction, or
    provider escalation is appropriate.

4.  Check applicable privacy obligations and any deadlines. Verify the
    requester's authority where required without collecting excessive
    identity documents.

5.  Explain the action taken or the reason further review is needed,
    subject to lawful limits on disclosure.

6.  Record the outcome and consider whether a wider technical or
    editorial change is needed to prevent recurrence.

## Handling a suspected security issue

1.  Receive the report through the private security channel and
    acknowledge it where practical.

2.  Assess severity and scope without unnecessarily reproducing harmful
    activity or accessing real user data.

3.  Apply temporary mitigations if needed, preserve relevant logs
    securely, and avoid publishing exploit details while the issue
    remains unresolved.

4.  Develop and test a fix in a controlled environment. Review related
    components for the same weakness.

5.  Deploy the fix, confirm that the affected behaviour is resolved, and
    communicate responsibly with the reporter.

6.  Review lessons learned and update security controls, documentation,
    and staff guidance.

## SEO and content maintenance cycle

1.  Review pages for usefulness, accuracy, originality, and whether the
    page still satisfies the visitor's search intent.

2.  Check indexability, canonical URLs, redirects, internal links,
    sitemap entries, and structured data.

3.  Improve pages with genuine missing information rather than adding
    repetitive keywords or filler.

4.  Check mobile usability, accessibility, page speed, and media
    optimisation.

5.  Use privacy-conscious analytics and search performance data to
    identify problems, not to manufacture misleading engagement.

6.  Record substantive updates and revisit important pages after major
    changes in the topic or its authoritative sources.

## ❓ Frequently asked questions

### Can I copy an article for my own website?

Not without permission from the relevant rights holder or a valid legal
basis. You may link to the public page. If you want to quote or reuse
material, keep the amount and purpose within any applicable legal
exception and provide attribution where required. Ask for written
permission when uncertain.

### Can I share a link to PUBLIC-WEBSITE?

Generally, sharing a link to a public page is different from copying the
page itself. Do not frame the site in a misleading way, bypass access
controls, or imply that PUBLIC-WEBSITE endorses your content.

### Can I take a screenshot?

A screenshot may reproduce protected text, images, layout, or other
material. Personal, private, educational, or commentary uses can raise
different legal questions depending on the context and jurisdiction. Do
not assume every screenshot may be republished. Seek permission for
public redistribution where needed.

### Does educational purpose mean I can reuse everything?

No. Educational purpose alone does not automatically authorise copying.
Copyright exceptions differ by jurisdiction and depend on factors such
as purpose, amount, nature of the work, and market effect. Check the
applicable law.

### What if my personal content appears without permission?

Save the URL and relevant evidence, avoid public escalation that exposes
more private information, and contact the relevant site or platform. You
may consider legal advice or formal remedies under applicable law.

### Does PUBLIC-WEBSITE own every logo or image shown?

No blanket ownership claim is made. Third-party logos, images, software,
and embedded media remain subject to their respective rights and
licences. Each asset should be checked individually.

### Will every support request receive an immediate reply?

No fixed response time is promised unless a separate agreement says so.
Provide clear details through the published support route and do not
include passwords or secrets.

### Can PUBLIC-WEBSITE guarantee a Google ranking?

No. Search engines control their ranking systems and may change them.
The site can follow sound technical and editorial practices, but no
ranking, indexing, or traffic result is guaranteed.

### Can a user submit a guest article?

Only if submissions are enabled and the applicable contributor terms are
provided. A contributor must have the rights needed to submit all text
and media.

### Can the website change this README?

The operator may update it as the site evolves. Changes should be
versioned and dated. Additional terms may apply to particular features
or agreements.

### Is this README a complete privacy policy?

No. Publish a separate privacy policy based on actual data flows,
service providers, cookies, forms, logs, analytics, and applicable legal
requirements.

### Can I use the PUBLIC-WEBSITE name or logo?

Do not use the branding in a way that suggests official status,
sponsorship, or endorsement without permission. Contact the brand owner
for proposed uses.

### What should I do if I find a broken link?

Send the page URL and the broken destination to the support contact. Do
not submit passwords, private account data, or unnecessary personal
information.

### Can I test the site's security?

Only within an explicitly authorised scope. Do not scan, exploit,
disrupt, or access data without permission. Use the private security
reporting channel for suspected issues.

### What if a page contains incorrect information?

Report the exact URL, explain the concern, and provide reliable
supporting sources if available. The site may review and correct the
page.

### Are external websites controlled by PUBLIC-WEBSITE?

No. External sites have their own operators, terms, and privacy
practices. Review them before downloading or submitting information.

### Does this document replace a signed contract?

No. A properly formed, applicable written agreement may govern a
specific project or service. Obtain legal review before relying on
contract language.

### Can I translate or adapt an article?

Translation and adaptation can involve copyright rights. Obtain
permission unless an applicable licence or legal exception permits the
use.

### Can I use code snippets commercially?

Check the licence for the exact snippet and any dependencies. If no
licence is stated, do not assume commercial reuse is allowed.

### What should I do before downloading a file?

Check the source, file type, purpose, and permissions. Scan files using
reputable security tools and test code in an isolated environment.
